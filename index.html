Symposium Abstract

Living Networks, including societies, groups and also brains, are continuously reshaping their structure, interactions, and information flow among nodes – yet their large-scale structure and function remain largely invariant. Classic examples are scale-free network properties and robust information processing despite variable neural representation.

We want to explore whether these networks are shaped by similar self-organization principles, show similar emergence of robustness and resilience, and are potentially also vulnerable to similar manipulation mechanisms.

By combining the perspective of complex systems with expertise from sociology and neuroscience, our aim is to identify common principles, but also core differences, that drive the emergence of structure, dynamics, and information flow – from neural networks to societal dynamics.

About the Symposium

From November 30 to December 2, 2026, at the Manfred-Eigen-Hörsaal (Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization, Göttingen), we bring together international experts across disciplines – including physics, data science, neuroscience, law, and social and political sciences.

With invited keynote and contributed talks, poster presentations, and ample time for interdisciplinary discussion, we aim to sharpen our understanding of how self-organization mechanisms drive structure and information flow in social networks as well as neural networks – with direct implications for understanding disinformation, polarization, and societal resilience.

The symposium is organized by the CIDAS Section “Complexity, Information, Cognition, Society” (CICS), supported and funded by the University of Göttingen, and aims to foster future collaborative research initiatives across disciplines.
