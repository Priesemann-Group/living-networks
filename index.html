Living Networks: Self-Organization, Manipulation and Information Flow in Neural and Social Systems

How do brains and societies remain robust while their underlying networks constantly reshape? We bring together perspectives from complex systems, neuroscience and social sciences to explore common principles of self-organization, resilience and manipulation across living networks.

Symposium abstract

Living networks – from neural circuits to online communities – continually reconfigure their structure, interactions and information flow, while large-scale function often remains remarkably stable. Classic examples include scale-free network architectures and robust information processing despite variable neural representations.

We ask whether similar self-organization principles shape these systems, whether robustness and resilience emerge through comparable mechanisms, and how they can be manipulated or disrupted. By combining complex systems theory with sociology, neuroscience and data science, we aim to identify shared principles and crucial differences that govern the emergence of structure, dynamics and information flow in neural and social networks.

At a glance

Why “Living Networks”?

Networked systems in the brain and in society adapt continuously. New connections are formed, existing ones are weakened, and information pathways are redirected — yet coherent function and behaviour often persist. Understanding how such robustness emerges, and how it can fail, is central to addressing challenges around disinformation, polarization and societal resilience.

This symposium creates a space where theorists, modellers and empirical researchers can speak across disciplinary boundaries, and jointly examine how living networks self-organize, process information and respond to intervention.
